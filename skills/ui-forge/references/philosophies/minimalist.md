# Minimalist

Less material, more intent. Every element earns its place or it leaves.

## Principles

- One idea per screen. Nothing on a page that does not carry product
  meaning.
- White space is structure, not emptiness: generous margins *are* the
  hierarchy.
- Max two fonts, one accent, one outstanding element per view.

## Structure

- One content column, generous gutters, margin-driven vertical rhythm.
- Navigation minimal: few top-level items, nothing decorative.
- Blocks separated by space and alignment, never by borders everywhere.

## Visual treatment

- Flat or near-flat surfaces; one accent, in use only for the focal path
  (one button, selected state, active link).
- Photography in mute, generous crops; zero clutter textures.
- Elevation: one subtle shadow level if any — mostly "flat" plus
  spacing.

## Typography and palette

- 1–2 families, few weights (regular + one medium/bold).
- Neutral greys, one hue of accent (e.g. `#2563eb`-class), big type
  stepping on a clean scale.
- Ample line-height; type as the ornament.

## Microinteractions

- Minimal: 100–180 ms fades/translates on hover and focus only; nothing
  injects motion for decoration.
- Press = subtle opacity/scale press, no bounce grammar.

## Dials (1–10)

- `variance`: 1–3 (deliberate uniformity of surface; variety comes from
  content, not decoration)
- `motion`: 1–3
- `density`: 1–4 (airy by default; densify only for tool views)

## Performance cost

- Lowest. ≤ 1 webfont-weight, near-zero JS for styling, no heavy
  shadows/filters, no image stacks. Shines at 60 KB critical.
- Skip decorative imagery; SVG + CSS covers it.

## Works best when

- Brand, product tour, landing, editorial "less is our luxury", B2B
  credibility play, a settings/utility product wanting calm.

## Avoid when

- A data-dense product needs to feel efficient (the density dial fights
  minimalism).
- The brand's personality is warmth/play (reads as cold or generic).
- You need to *prove* the product is packed with features at first
  glance.
